I asked Claude to come up with a set of "rules of thumb", based on some readings I had available. It's a pretty good set, and it provides details of where the evidence and arguments come from. As with any list of "guidelines" view them as suggestions/evidence, not necessarily hard rules. 

<!--more-->

What is below is from Claude. 

This was "almost" one shotted with a minimal prompt. It was done in stages (e.g., I asked it to process in batches). I had collected the material locally (so it didn't have to do lots of web fetches), including summaries of scans of books.

{{<expand "The prompts (in case you are curious)">}}
I did this with Claude Sonnet 5, in mid-September, 2026. I had intended to use Opus. There was a lot of context (which is hard to describe).

1. extract a list of potential guidelines. try to group similar ones (where different sources say similar things). be sure to provide references to where things come from. the goal is not necessarily to make an exhaustive list - but rather to get a list of a lot of common guidance. before starting: this is a potentially infinite, open ended quest. let's choose what the boundary is for an initial exploration so we can then decide to do more. Is it possible to bound the quest by a time budget (or LLM cost budget)?
 
2. specific instructions for it to make a checklist/queue of documents to look at and process in batches. "Also, do not go to extensive effort to obtain difficult to obtain sources. For example, many of the book chapters and papers I may have access to through means that you do not. I think this means a third category in the "queue" checklist: documents that you would like to read, but you think they may take too much effort to find."

3. various "do the next N" 

4. "Also (probably should have asked this at the start): be sure to note where there are disagreements"
